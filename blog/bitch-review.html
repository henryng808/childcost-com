The Bitch: Grief, Guilt, and the Mothers We Become

ChildCost Editorial Team
September 2, 2026 • 9 min read

Released September 1, 2026, The Bitch by Pilar Quintana (Riverhead) — "a perfect book," in Kiran Desai's words — follows a grieving mother on the Colombian Pacific coast who pours her love into a stray dog after the loss of her daughter. In her need to mother again, she confronts the rawest questions of parenthood: what we are trying to replace when we care, and whether love can ever be enough.

The Grief Premium

Quintana identifies the "grief premium" — the price parents pay when love has nowhere safe to go. The novel's unnamed mother turns her impossible longing toward a creature that cannot save her, and in that turning, Quintana maps the exact geography of maternal guilt: the feeling that you failed, that you should have protected more, that caring again is a betrayal and a necessity at once. For every parent who has ever cared through grief, The Bitch is a mirror held up with terrifying tenderness.

Lessons for Families:

  • Where Love Goes When It Hurts: Understanding how grief re-routes the care instinct — and why that is human, not shameful.
  • Maternal Guilt as Geography: Naming the impossible standards mothers carry after loss.
  • Caring as Survival: How the need to mother can be both wound and raft.

The ChildCost Verdict

The Bitch is a perfect, devastating book — one that every parent who has grieved will recognize in their bones. Pilar Quintana writes with a spareness that leaves nothing to hide behind.
