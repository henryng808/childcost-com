The Strangers: A New Species of Writing About Grief

ChildCost Editorial Team
September 26, 2026 • 10 min read

Released September 22, 2026, The Strangers by Naomi Alderman (Little Brown) is the new novel from the bestselling author of The Power. Eleanor Catton calls it "an entirely new species of writing about grief. I loved this weird, warm-blooded, and inquisitive book." For families, grief is the guest that arrives without warning and never quite leaves — and Alderman's novel is one of the few that treats it as something to be curious about rather than merely endured.

The Curiosity Premium

Alderman identifies the "curiosity premium" — the strange gift that arrives when a family stops trying to resolve loss and starts asking what it is. Her book is warm-blooded where grief books are usually cold: it investigates instead of mourning, and in doing so it makes room for the living. In a 2026 where parents are told endlessly to optimize childhood, The Strangers is a bracing reminder that some of the most important family work is simply paying attention to what hurts.

Lessons for Families:

  • Grief Has a Shape: Learning to be curious about loss instead of frightened by it.
  • Warm-Blooded Inquiry: How families can investigate pain together rather than around each other.
  • Room for the Living: Making space for joy and grief to coexist in the same household.

The ChildCost Verdict

The Strangers is weird, warm-blooded, and genuinely new — a grief novel that refuses to be solemn and is all the more moving for it. Naomi Alderman proves once again that she writes the future of fiction.
