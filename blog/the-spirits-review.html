The Spirits: Vengeance, Grief, and Radiant Truth

ChildCost Editorial Team
October 19, 2026 • 9 min read

Released October 13, 2026, The Spirits by Bonnie Jo Campbell (W. W. Norton) is, in Booklist's assessment, written in prose that is "rapturous and darn near Shakespearean as she conjures storms of vengeance and grief that give way to the radiance of truth and love." Grief is the family cost no calculator includes — and the one that most often determines what a household can afford to feel, forgive, and attempt next.

The Grief Ledger

Campbell works a territory most financial writing avoids: the "grief ledger," the unpaid, unmeasured labor of carrying loss inside a family. In a year of childcare-market collapses and shuttered centres, families are absorbing a second kind of grief — the loss of the place their child was known. The Spirits is a reminder that grief has a temperature, and that children read it long before they can name it.

Lessons for Families:

  • Naming the Loss: Why grief that is spoken aloud lands differently than grief that is managed silently.
  • Vengeance or Repair: The fork every hurt family reaches — and what choosing repair actually costs.
  • Radiance After Ruin: How truth and love become available again once the storm has been allowed to pass through.

The ChildCost Verdict

The Spirits is a fierce, generous novel — the kind that makes a family's hardest season feel witnessed rather than wasted.
