July 2026 in Review: The Month Childcare Became Macroeconomic Infrastructure

ChildCost Editorial Team
July 31, 2026 • 8 min read

July 2026 will be remembered as the month that childcare broke through from social policy to macroeconomic infrastructure. Across the IMF, World Bank, OECD, UNICEF, and every major multilateral institution, the message was the same: the care economy is not a family problem — it is the fundamental infrastructure of the post-AI labor market.

The Month in Numbers

  • $23.6 billion: Total childcare core financing mobilized by Multilateral Development Banks — a 67% increase since 2018.
  • $13.3 million: World Bank catalytic grants deployed to 73 projects across 64 countries.
  • 23.4 million: Additional children at risk of poverty by end of 2026, per UNICEF.
  • 29%: Caregivers now part of the "sandwich generation" — simultaneously responsible for children and aging parents.
  • 3.3%: IMF global growth projection, with childcare explicitly cited as a structural growth determinant for the first time in WEO history.

The Paradigm Shift

From the G7 Care Workforce Summit to the graduation of 13 countries from the World Bank's Early Childhood Policy Academy, July 2026 represented a turning point. The question is no longer whether to invest in childcare — it's how fast the infrastructure can be built. At ChildCost, we'll be tracking every development. August, we're ready.
