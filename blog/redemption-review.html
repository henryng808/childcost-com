Redemption: Faith, Justice, and the Women Who Carry It

ChildCost Editorial Team
October 26, 2026 • 10 min read

Released October 20, 2026, Redemption: Faith, Justice, and Sisterhood by Lawrence Wright (Knopf) is, in Belle Burden's words, a book that "gripped me like the best crime novel and left me fundamentally changed." Wright — the Pulitzer Prize-winning author of The Looming Tower — turns his investigative eye to women bound by faith and family, and to the question that quietly structures so much unpaid care: who is expected to hold a community together, and at what personal cost. In a month when the country learned that most home-based child care providers are over 50, this book reads less like history and more like a map of who has been doing the holding.

The Sisterhood Ledger

What Wright documents is a ledger every family pays into and rarely audits: the labor of women whose care work is framed as devotion, and therefore never invoiced. It is the same ledger that keeps a home-based provider in Washington, D.C., at 67, still opening her doors at dawn with no retirement plan and no successor. Wright gives that labor its dignity and its accounting.

Lessons for Families:

  • Name the Unpaid Ledger: Track the care that never appears in a household budget — and notice who is carrying it.
  • Sisterhood as Infrastructure: Why informal networks of women are the load-bearing structure beneath schools, churches and child care.
  • Succession, Not Inspiration: The hardest question in any care story is not who starts it, but who inherits it.

The ChildCost Verdict

Redemption is narrative nonfiction at its most useful: a book that makes care work visible, and in doing so changes how you read your own family's history.
