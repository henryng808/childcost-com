Freyja: Margrét Ann Thors on a Mother's Love and a Child's Disappearance

ChildCost Editorial Team
August 12, 2026 • 10 min read

Released August 11, 2026 from Spiegel & Grau, Freyja is the debut novel by Margrét Ann Thors — a gripping mystery steeped in Icelandic folklore about a girl who disappeared many years ago. At its heart is Unnur, a loving mother whose life has been shadowed by loss. Literary Hub named it among the most anticipated books of the year, praising its rare fusion of myth and motherhood.

The Mother Love Premium

Thors identifies the "mother love premium" — the fierce, unceasing form of care that survives absence, uncertainty, and even the longest years. Woven through with Icelandic folklore and myth, Freyja is at once a story of a child's disappearance, a testament to the power of a mother's love, and an exploration of how the stories we tell about our children shape the ways we hold them. The novel asks what it means to keep loving — and searching — when all the evidence says to stop.

Lessons for Families:

  • Love That Endures: The mother's love that outlasts absence and uncertainty.
  • Myth as Meaning: How folklore and family stories give shape to grief and hope.
  • The Search That Never Ends: The power of refusing to give up on the ones we love.

The ChildCost Verdict

Freyja is a luminous, myth-rich debut. Thors proves that a mother's love is the most persistent force in any family story — and that the search for a child is never really over.
