Anti-Fascist Economics: Why Affordability Is a Family Issue

ChildCost Editorial Team
October 26, 2026 • 11 min read

Released October 20, 2026, Anti-Fascist Economics: In Defense of Affordability, Dignity, and Democracy by Isabella Weber (Random House) arrives with a blurb from Lina M. Khan calling it "an urgent reminder of how economic precarity threatens democracy" that "masterfully explains why we must focus on securing the material preconditions of freedom." Weber, the economist known for her work on price shocks and strategic price controls, makes an argument that lands directly on the family budget: the price of a thing your child cannot do without is not merely an economic variable. It is a political one.

The Affordability Premium

For ChildCost readers, Weber's framework reframes the childcare crisis. When licensed home-based providers age out, when fees outpace rent, when a center closes and never reopens, the resulting pressure is not a market inefficiency to be tolerated. It is a transfer of freedom away from families. Weber's insistence on affordability as a precondition of dignity gives parents precise language for what they are already feeling at the kitchen table — and a standard against which to judge any policy that claims to help.

Lessons for Families:

  • Price as Politics: Why the sticker price of care is a decision someone made, not weather that happened.
  • The Cost of Precarity: How chronic unaffordability erodes the bandwidth parents need to plan, save, and advocate.
  • A Test for Policy: One question to ask any childcare proposal: does it lower the price, or merely rename it?

The ChildCost Verdict

Anti-Fascist Economics is demanding but essential reading — the rare book that hands parents an economic vocabulary equal to the decisions they are forced to make.
