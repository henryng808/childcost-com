Things Don't Break on Their Own: The Architecture of Damage

ChildCost Editorial Team
July 27, 2026 • 10 min read

Selected as one of Good Housekeeping's "50 Best Ever Summer Reads," Things Don't Break on Their Own by Sarah Easter Collins is a gripping novel of domestic suspense that probes the deepest question facing every family: where does the damage come from? Collins layers a story of friendship, community, and long-buried secrets into a narrative that insists on one truth — things don't break on their own. Broken people were broken by something or someone.

The Inheritance of Silence

Collins identifies the "silence premium" — the compounding cost of the secrets families keep, which echo across generations. In a 2026 world where mental health awareness has finally entered the mainstream but intergenerational trauma remains stubbornly hidden, Things Don't Break on Their Own is essential reading. The novel layers past and present, showing how damage inflicted in one decade shapes the relationships of the next.

Lessons for Families:

  • The Causality of Care: Understanding that emotional damage has a source — and healing requires looking upstream.
  • Friendship as Family Infrastructure: How deep friendships become the scaffolding that holds families together in crisis.
  • Truth as Repair: The slow, patient work of breaking generational cycles through radical honesty.

The ChildCost Verdict

Things Don't Break on Their Own is a masterfully crafted novel that belongs on every family's shelf. Collins reminds us that the invisible damage of family secrets is a cost that compounds — and that the truth, no matter how painful, is the only path to repair.
