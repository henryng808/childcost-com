An Unexpected Life: How We Survive, How We Care

ChildCost Editorial Team
September 26, 2026 • 10 min read

Released September 22, 2026, An Unexpected Life by Gloria Steinem (Random House) is the final book from a writer who spent six decades insisting that the personal is political. Roxane Gay calls it "a fiercely beautiful book about how we survive, how we learn, how we keep faith with one another, and how an unexpected life can become a formidable force for freedom and connection." For families, it is a handbook of caregiving: Steinem's own girlhood was shaped by looking after a mother who could not care for herself — the unpaid labor that still underpins the American family, and that our economy still refuses to count.

The Caregiving Premium

Steinem identifies the "caregiving premium" — the invisible product of all the looking-after that families do: the meals, the appointments, the patience, the years. In a 2026 where Alaska's advocates put it plainly — "childcare is a public good: it needs a public subsidy" — her book is the human proof behind the policy argument. Care work is not a private burden. It is the infrastructure that lets every other form of work happen.

Lessons for Families:

  • Care Is Infrastructure: Why the unpaid work of raising and tending is the economy's real foundation.
  • Keeping Faith With One Another: How families survive on reciprocity rather than sacrifice alone.
  • The Unexpected Life: Steinem's reminder that no family's script is fixed — an unplanned life can become a force for freedom.

The ChildCost Verdict

An Unexpected Life is a fiercely beautiful, necessary book — the parting argument of a great American writer that care is the work that holds everything else up. Families who feel unseen by policy will feel seen by Steinem.
