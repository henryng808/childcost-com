Dèy: The Language of Home and the Weight of Roots

ChildCost Editorial Team
August 26, 2026 • 9 min read

Released August 25, 2026, Dèy by Edwidge Danticat (Knopf) is — as NPR writes — a work that "truly captures the Haitian American immigrant experience with prose that is so languid and all-consuming that one never wants to be released from its grasp." In Haitian Creole, "dèy" means both "behind" and "after" — a word that holds the whole geography of family: what we carry from where we came, and what we leave for those who come after.

The Roots Premium

Danticat identifies the "roots premium" — the cost and value of maintaining family connection across oceans, languages, and generations. Immigrant families in particular face a double economy: the financial cost of care and remittances, and the emotional cost of translation — between generations, between countries, between the home you left and the home you are building. In a 2026 world of migration, climate displacement, and separated families, Dèy is an essential meditation on what we owe our origins and what our children owe us.

Lessons for Families:

  • Language as Home: How mother tongue and family stories anchor identity across generations.
  • The Double Economy: Naming both the financial and emotional costs of maintaining family across borders.
  • Legacy of Place: Teaching children that roots are not a burden — they are the soil of belonging.

The ChildCost Verdict

Dèy is a luminous, essential work from one of the great writers of family and diaspora. Edwidge Danticat proves that the strongest families are those that keep translating love across every border — and keep coming home, even when home is a word they carry with them.
