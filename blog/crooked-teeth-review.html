Crooked Teeth: A Queer Syrian Refugee's Memoir of Family

ChildCost Editorial Team
August 27, 2026 • 10 min read

Released August 25, 2026, Crooked Teeth: A Queer Syrian Refugee Memoir by Danny Ramadan (Soft Skull) is a memoir of survival that is also, at its heart, a family story: what it means to be loved and loved imperfectly across war, exile, and the hardest act of all — coming home to yourself. Ramadan, one of the most acclaimed chroniclers of the Syrian diaspora, writes with a tenderness that turns displacement into inheritance and queerness into a kind of family heirloom.

The Belonging Premium

Ramadan identifies the "belonging premium" — the double cost paid by children who must reconcile the family they were born into with the family they must become. For refugee families, that cost is compounded by language, citizenship, and the constant work of translation between worlds. In a 2026 world where one in every 69 people is displaced, Crooked Teeth is an essential meditation on what children inherit from exile — and what they are free to rebuild.

Lessons for Families:

  • Imperfect Love: Accepting that the families we are born into love us crookedly — and that crooked love can still hold.
  • Translation as Care: The daily labor of carrying family identity between languages, countries, and generations.
  • Becoming the Heirloom: Choosing which parts of a family's story to keep, and which to bravely let go.

The ChildCost Verdict

Crooked Teeth is a triumph of memoir — a book about family that refuses to pretend family is simple. Danny Ramadan proves that the strongest families are the ones that survive the truth, one crooked smile at a time.
