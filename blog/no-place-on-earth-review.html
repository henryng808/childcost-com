No Place on Earth: When Home Must Be Made Anew

ChildCost Editorial Team
August 13, 2026 • 9 min read

Named among LitHub's 10 Great New Children's Books of August 2026, No Place on Earth (Random House) is a powerful new work about the most fundamental human question: what do we do when the place we call home disappears? In an era of climate displacement, housing precarity, and family relocation, this children's book gives young readers a framework for one of the hardest experiences a family can face — losing home — and the courage it takes to make home anew.

The Home-Making Premium

The author identifies the "home-making premium" — the profound resilience children gain when they learn that home is not a place but an act: something we build, carry, and recreate together. In a 2026 world where millions of families are being displaced by climate events and economic pressure, No Place on Earth is an essential conversation-starter for parents navigating relocation, upheaval, or simply the question of what home means.

Lessons for Families:

  • Home as Practice: Teaching children that belonging is something we build, not just inherit.
  • Resilience Through Change: Framing displacement as a challenge families can meet together.
  • The Portable Sanctuary: How rituals and routines let children carry safety wherever they go.

The ChildCost Verdict

No Place on Earth is a tender, essential children's book for our unsettled era. It proves that the most important thing a parent can give a displaced child is not a place — it is the knowledge that home travels with them, in the hearts they carry.
