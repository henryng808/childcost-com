Notice and Do: The Chore Chart Was Never the Problem

ChildCost Editorial Team
October 27, 2026 • 8 min read

Released October 20, 2026 by HarperOne, Notice and Do: Raise Capable Kids, End Motherhood Burnout, and Lighten Your Mental Load by Sam Kelly takes aim at the object most often offered as a solution to household labor: the chore chart. Kelly, a parenting coach whose "notice and do" method spread through her work with families, argues that charts fail for a structural reason. They teach children to complete assigned tasks, not to perceive what needs doing. The noticing — the scanning, sequencing, and remembering that keeps a household running — stays exactly where it was.

The Part You Cannot Outsource

The distinction matters more than it sounds. A child who empties the dishwasher when told has learned compliance. A child who notices the dishwasher is clean and empties it has learned ownership of a shared space. Kelly builds the book around that gap, showing how to move children from waiting for instructions to reading a room — a skill she argues works with young children and with neurodivergent kids, precisely because it does not depend on remembering a rotating list.

For families weighing the price of a child, this is the invisible line item. Childcare dollars buy hours of coverage. They do not buy the noticing. Every hour of coverage a family purchases still leaves someone holding the schedule, the appointments, the school forms, and the mental inventory of what runs out next. That load is not a feeling; it is unpaid work, and it is the reason so many parents report exhaustion that a night out does not fix.

Lessons for Families:

  • Transfer Attention, Not Tasks: The goal is a child who sees the full kitchen, not one who completes a checklist and leaves.
  • Name the Load Out Loud: Kelly's method starts with parents making visible the scanning work they do silently all day.
  • Start Smaller Than Feels Reasonable: Capability builds from one repeated noticing habit, not from a Saturday of delegated chores.

The ChildCost Verdict

Notice and Do is the rare parenting book that treats household labor as an economic question rather than a moral one. It will not lower your childcare bill, but it addresses the cost that never appears on one.
