The Story of the Body: Poems of Illness and Recovery

ChildCost Editorial Team
October 26, 2026 • 9 min read

Released October 20, 2026, The Story of the Body: Poems of Illness and Recovery by Meghan O'Rourke (W. W. Norton) is, per Ocean Vuong, "essential reading for all." O'Rourke is the poet and essayist whose earlier work on grief, chronic illness and caregiving established her as one of the most exact writers we have about the body in trouble. This collection turns that precision on recovery — the unglamorous, ongoing labor of getting well, which in most households is performed by family members who never trained for it.

The Caregiving Premium

There is a hidden line item in every family budget that no calculator captures: the hours spent in waiting rooms, on hold with insurers, driving to appointments, and learning a medication schedule. O'Rourke's poems give those hours a form. For parents, the collection carries a specific charge — caretaking is what we do in both directions, for our children and for our parents, sometimes in the same afternoon. The book refuses to sentimentalize that, and is more moving for it.

Lessons for Families:

  • Recovery Is Labor: Treat convalescence as work with a schedule, not a state of waiting.
  • Legible Care: Why naming what a caregiver does makes it easier to ask for help before burnout.
  • Reading Aloud: These poems are short enough to share — a way to open a conversation about illness that families often postpone.

The ChildCost Verdict

The Story of the Body is spare, exact, and quietly consoling — the kind of book a family keeps on the shelf where the medical files are.
